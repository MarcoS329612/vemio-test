hypothesis reaches `supported`, its conclusion is promoted here.

### F-017
**The `discount` column and its two related fields carry three independent defects, not one — consolidated here because they were discovered separately but compound on the same SKU**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02 through 2026-08-04, across Phases 1, 2 and 4. Consolidated
  2026-08-04 during the Task 10 registry close-out, on the judgement that a reader tracing
  "what is wrong with the discount/price fields" should find one entry rather than three
  scattered ones. The three underlying findings (F-003, F-004, F-015) are **not
  superseded** — their evidence and phase-specific impact stay where they are; this entry
  is the index a reader should start from.
- **Severity**: High
- **Evidence** — three separate defects, each in a different field:
  1. **`product_cost` carries the margin backwards (F-003).** `product_cost/bruto − 1` is
     exactly constant per SKU and reproduces the documented 0.20–0.30 markup band, but only
     if `unit_cost = bruto/(1 + margin)` — the reverse of `product_cost` taken literally,
     which would make every one of 358,775 rows loss-making. Now defended by an executable
     check, not only an argument (**DR-0006**, `quality.check_margin_convention`): on 414
     free-goods lines, the adopted reading gives **−420,300** margin (correct sign) against
     **+648,300** under the rejected reading (wrong sign — giveaways would be the most
     profitable transactions in the dataset).
  2. **`bruto` does not reflect combo-level discounts, so the weekly panel's
     `discount_depth` proxy is blind to them (F-004, F-015).** Combo 9590 has
     `bruto == sell_in_amount` on 99.6% of its 1,367 lines — the proxy reads zero discount —
     while `discount` itself records a constant 0.20 on the same lines. For SKUs 9304,
     1857 and 1858 the panel proxy reads roughly a tenth of the depth `discount` records
     (`reports/04_elasticity.md` §6). The two fields answer different questions and are not
     interchangeable.
  3. **`discount` is negative on 2.81% of rows (10,084 of 358,775), and that block is not
     spread evenly — it concentrates overwhelmingly on one SKU.** Re-checked directly
     against the raw CSV for this consolidation (not re-derived from a cached artifact):
     **9,928 of the 10,084 negative-discount rows (98.5%) belong to SKU 1283** — 8.33% of
     that SKU's own 119,133 rows, against 0.23% for 1875 and 0.03% for 1665, and
     effectively none for 1857, 1858 or 9304. This was previously reported only as a
     dataset-wide 2.81% (F-004); the concentration on a single SKU is new here.
- **Impact**: These are three unrelated failure modes that happen to share a family of
  columns, and a reader who only saw the dataset-wide 2.81% negative-discount figure would
  reasonably assume it was diffuse. It is not: SKU 1283 already carries the largest
  disclosed complication in `reports/04_elasticity.md` §6 (29.07% of its promoted-line
  units have a null `discount`, imputed to zero); the concentration found here means the
  *same SKU* also holds nearly all the negative-discount rows dataset-wide. Anyone working
  with 1283's discount field specifically is compounding two separate data-quality issues,
  not one — worth knowing before trusting a break-even or elasticity figure for that SKU
  more than the disclosed caveats already suggest.
- **Status**: closed (consolidation); the underlying open items are unchanged — Q5 (margin
  direction) stays open, Q6 (negative discount) stays open

### F-012
**Warehouses differ in kind, not only in size**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-04, Phase 2 (stage 02 EDA, commercial context)
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Evidence**: Revenue concentrates hard — the top 2 of 12 warehouses hold **44.07%** of
  revenue, the top 5 hold **74.03%**. Cubito de pollo (`1283`) is the top-revenue product in
  **all 12 warehouses**. But revenue per client separates them further than the revenue
  ranking alone: bodega n. 3 nets **1,884** per client against bodega n. 8's **299** —
  roughly 6x — while both serve a few thousand clients. Dataset shape confirmed again in
  this pass: 12 warehouses, **228 routes**, **52,555 clients**.
- **Impact**: Stage 06 splits national stock by revenue share per warehouse. A warehouse
  with few, high-value clients (bodega n. 3) carries a different stock-out cost profile
  than one with many low-value clients (bodega n. 6, 15,562 clients) at a comparable share
  — worth carrying into any future refinement of the allocation rule.
- **Status**: closed

### F-013
**Warehouse 11's shutdown is a gradual, 8-month wind-down inside the training window, not a data cut**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-04, Phase 2 (stage 02 EDA, commercial context)
- **Severity**: High
- **Evidence**: Every warehouse except one sells through the last observed week
  (2026-05-29/30). Warehouse 11's last sale is **2025-08-28**. Its monthly ticket count
  tapers rather than stopping abruptly: 324 (Jan 2025) → 256 → 370 → 313 → 237 → 147 → 32 →
  **9** (Aug 2025) → 0 for the remaining ~9.5 months of history.
- **Impact**: This is a structural break sitting inside Challenge A's training window, not
  at its edge. A model that does not know warehouse 11 exited reads the taper as a demand
  collapse rather than an operational exit, and any warehouse-level or per-warehouse-share
  feature built across the full history will misread it. Stage 03's forecast caveats should
  say so explicitly. Stage 06 already excludes warehouse 11 from the allocation share base
  (see `reports/06_allocation.md` §1) — this finding is the evidence for that exclusion,
  published for the first time here.
- **Status**: closed

### F-014
**The median client buys 3 times across 74 weeks and 1 SKU per ticket**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-04, Phase 2 (stage 02 EDA, commercial context)
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Evidence**: Across 52,555 clients, median tickets over the 74-week history is **3**
  (p90 **12**); **51.8%** of clients bought 3 times or fewer. Median distinct SKUs per
  ticket is **1**, and **73.4%** of tickets (**200,832** of 273,778) carry exactly one SKU.
  All four figures are printed directly in `reports/02_eda.md` §9's key-value table, not
  hand-copied — a round-1 review caught a hand-typed variant of this count (200,918) that
  did not reproduce under any population variant tried; the script now emits the ticket
  total and the single-SKU count explicitly so this entry can always be traced to a
  printed value.
- **Impact**: A typical customer is a thin, infrequent signal. Reading promotional uplift
  per customer would be almost all noise; it has to be read at the network level —
  aggregate weekly units — which is how every uplift estimate in stage 05 is already
  constructed. This finding is the reason that design choice is correct, published
  explicitly for the first time here.
- **Status**: closed

### F-015
**Discount depth clusters on discrete levels, has a distinct 100%-off bonus-product group, and has only a modest, not negligible, client effect**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-04, Phase 2 (stage 02 EDA, commercial context); revised
  2026-08-04 after round-1 review
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Evidence**: Using the delivered `discount` column (not the panel's `discount_depth`
  proxy, which is blind to combo-level discounts — e.g. combo 9590 has
  `bruto == sell_in_amount` on 99.6% of 1,367 lines while `discount` reads a constant 0.2),
  restricted to promoted lines via `economics.promo_discount_mask` (imported directly by
  `scripts/02_eda.py`, not duplicated): **158,941** lines carry a non-null value, over
  **1,465 distinct** discount values, but the mass clusters hard on a handful of levels —
  0.14 (26,972 lines), 0.16 (26,593), 0.15 (14,856), 0.17 (14,034), 0.20 (12,397), 0.25
  (9,782), 0.00 (8,694), 0.22, 0.13, 0.19, 0.33, 0.21 — a discrete commercial ladder, not a
  continuum. Separately, **118 lines** carry exactly `discount == 1.0`; **100%** of them
  have zero net amount, i.e. free product (811 units given away), not a price cut.
  For client-specificity: among **3,248 clients** with 10+ discounted lines, the median
  within-client standard deviation of depth is **0.0566** against a between-client standard
  deviation (of client-level means) of **0.0332**. The claim that client explains less of
  the variation than timing/combo does is now measured directly, not inferred from the
  dispersion statistics alone: a one-way ANOVA on the same eligible-client subset gives
  **R² = 13.6%** for `client_code` against **R² = 52.2%** for `id_combo` — combo explains
  roughly 4x more of the variance in discount depth than client does. This client R² is
  sensitive to the minimum-discounted-lines threshold used to define "eligible" — it runs
  from **1.8%** at a 50-line cutoff up to **20.2%** at a 5-line cutoff — but the direction
  never flips across that range: combo always dominates client.
- **Impact**: Supports treating promotional depth as a largely centrally-set, network-wide
  lever rather than a per-customer negotiation — consistent with, and now evidence for,
  Challenge C's network-level uplift design (F-014) and any future promo-calendar
  recommendation. **Caveats, both from round-1 review**: (1) the within-client standard
  deviation (0.0566) is not literally negligible in absolute terms against a typical
  discount of ~0.17 — the defensible claim is the *relative* one (R² comparison above), not
  an absolute "near zero." (2) A 13.6% client R² is a real, if modest, effect — "essentially
  not client-specific" overstated it; "only a modest client effect, dominated by combo" is
  the claim the evidence supports, and it holds at every threshold tested (1.8%–20.2%).
- **Status**: closed

### F-016
**No warehouse was left untreated while others moved price — there is no warehouse-level control group for Challenge C**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-04, Phase 2 (stage 02 EDA, commercial context)
- **Severity**: High
- **Evidence**: Weekly realised price per warehouse for SKU 1665 (Nov 2025 – Mar 2026): in
  the week of **2026-02-02**, **10 of the 11** then-active warehouses (warehouse 11 had
  already shut down, F-013) drop realised price by 5% or more in the same week; at a 3%
  cutoff, **all 11** move together. Price moves are simultaneous across the network rather
  than staggered market by market.
- **Impact**: This rules out a warehouse-level difference-in-differences design for **this
  promotional episode** — there is no untreated warehouse to serve as a counterfactual
  during it. Stage 05 uses other SKUs as its control instead of other warehouses; that
  choice was made without publishing this diagnostic. Cross-referenced from
  `reports/05_uplift.md` §7 (What limits these numbers).
- **Scope**: This is direct evidence for one SKU over one price transition, not an
  exhaustive audit of every promotion in the dataset. Generalising it to "no warehouse-level
  control ever exists" is an inference resting on F-012 (pricing behaves as a centralised,
  network-wide decision, not a per-warehouse one) — reasonable, but not independently
  checked episode by episode.
- **Status**: closed

### F-008
**The same promotion, run on two comparable SKUs, made money on one and lost it on the other**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4 (uplift economics)
- **Severity**: High — it is the finding the promotional recommendation rests on
- **Evidence**: SKUs 1665 and 1875 both ran the same long-running promotion: 60 weeks each,
  ~14% realised discount each, ending at almost identical prices (51.06 and 51.07). Both
  produced large volume uplift — **+56,313** and **+54,372** net incremental units. Yet
  1665 nets **+42,311** in margin while 1875 nets **−76,315**. The entire difference is the
  cost base: margin per unit at the promoted price is **5.41** for 1665 against **2.37** for
  1875, because their recovered margin rates are 0.30 and 0.22.
- **Impact**: Volume uplift is not a measure of whether a promotion worked. Promotional
  depth has to be set against each product's own margin rather than applied uniformly
  across a portfolio. This drives business recommendation #1.
- **Confidence**: Both estimates are graded `weak` on identification — no clean control SKU
  existed in those weeks — so the magnitudes carry error bars. The *direction* of the
  contrast does not depend on the counterfactual, because both SKUs share it.
- **Status**: closed

### F-009
**The promotions that created margin were the ones with no discount at all**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4
- **Severity**: High
- **Evidence**: SKU 1283's March 2026 episode ran at a realised discount depth of **−0.0%**
  — a placement or bundling mechanic rather than a price cut — and produced **6,126 net
  incremental units** for **+235,626** in margin, the best result of the nine episodes
  measured. Discount-led episodes on the *same SKU* produced comparable volume
  (+6,155 and +2,736 units) but lost **−47,595** and **−67,479**, because the discount is
  paid on every unit sold in the window including the ones that would have sold anyway.
- **Impact**: Drives business recommendation #2 — build the calendar around non-price
  mechanics first and make depth discounting justify itself.
- **Confidence**: The March 2026 episode is graded `moderate` (clean baseline, observable
  post window, no control SKUs available). The pattern across episodes is consistent enough
  that the conclusion does not rest on any single one.
- **Status**: closed

### F-010
**No sophisticated model beat a 4-week moving average on two of the three forecast SKUs**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4/5
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Evidence**: Across five rolling origins at a 12-week horizon, the moving average won on
  SKU 1857 (WAPE **0.257**) and 1665 (**0.291**), beating ETS, harmonic ridge, seasonal
  naive and an ensemble. Only SKU 1283 supported a better model: damped drift at **0.272**
  against the best baseline's 0.322, a 15.5% improvement. Seasonal naive was among the worst
  performers everywhere (see H-003).
- **Impact**: Sets the realistic accuracy expectation for planning (~26–29% weekly error)
  and justifies not shipping a complex model. Also produced the diagnostic that led to the
  damped-drift candidate: every level-only model under-forecast by 13–30%, revealing an
  upward drift that flat forecasts could not follow.
- **Status**: closed

### F-011
**10% of SKU 1665's history was priced below break-even**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4 (simulator); **figure corrected 2026-08-04** during
  Task 10's reproduction check
- **Severity**: High
- **Evidence**: Under the reconstructed unit cost (46.30, from F-003), the simulator's
  margin curve crosses zero at a price of **46.41** (computed from the simulation grid
  itself, `reports/04_elasticity.md` §5 — not read off the coarser 10-point display table,
  which lands on 46.96 and is where this entry's earlier "about 47" came from). **7 of the
  72 weeks in the history (10%)** were priced below that line, with the deepest week at
  42.87 — an **−8% margin**. Demand is genuinely elastic (−4.73), so those weeks did sell
  more volume; each additional unit was sold at a loss.
- **Correction note**: this entry originally read "roughly a third of the 72 weeks."
  Re-checked against the regenerated `reports/04_elasticity.md` while closing this task's
  registries and found stale — the code computes `loss_weeks` directly from the data
  (`(data["price"] < break_even_price).sum()`) and prints the reproducible figure of 10%,
  not a third. The break-even *price* itself did not move materially (46.30–46.96 depending
  on which grid resolution is read); what moved was which figure had been hand-carried into
  this registry versus read from the live report. Business recommendations built on this
  finding now cite 10%, per the regenerated report.
- **Impact**: Gives the commercial team a hard price floor and drives a business
  recommendation. Also explains why the revenue-maximising price sits at the boundary of
  the observed range — a corner solution the simulator correctly refuses to extrapolate
  past (see DR-0007).
- **Caveat**: Inherits open question **Q5**. If VEMIO's cost reading differs, the break-even
  price moves; the elasticity and volume conclusions do not.
- **Status**: open pending Q5

### F-003
**`product_cost` sits above gross revenue on every row — the margin is applied in the wrong direction**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (cost/margin structure)
- **Severity**: **Critical** — taken literally, the client loses money on all 358,775 transactions
- **Evidence**: The ratio `product_cost / bruto` is **exactly constant within each SKU**
  (standard deviation 0.0 to nine decimals):

  | product_code | product_name | cost/bruto | implied margin | margin as literally delivered |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1283 | Cubito de pollo c/50 | 1.24 | 0.24 | **−19.4%** |
  | 1665 | Antitranspirante 150 ml C | 1.30 | 0.30 | **−23.1%** |
  | 1857 | Shampoo Rizos 135 ml | 1.27 | 0.27 | **−21.3%** |
  | 1858 | Shampoo 135 ml Azul | 1.26 | 0.26 | **−20.6%** |
  | 1875 | Desodorante 150 ml A | 1.22 | 0.22 | **−18.0%** |
  | 9304 | Shampoo 180ml Verde | 1.22 | 0.22 | **−18.0%** |

  The `cost/bruto − 1` column lands on 0.22–0.30 — precisely the "fixed markup per SKU,
  between 0.20 and 0.30, constant across the period" that the dictionary attributes to
  `product_margin`. The relationship in the file is `product_cost = bruto × (1 + margin)`,
  whereas a markup over cost implies `product_cost = bruto ÷ (1 + margin)`.
- **Impact**: Every margin figure in Challenge B depends on which reading is intended.
  Using the column as delivered would report a loss-making business and make the
  price-optimisation exercise meaningless; correcting it silently would hide a material
  assumption behind the headline recommendation.
- **Action taken**: Raised as open question **Q5** to VEMIO. Documented fallback: treat
  `product_margin = product_cost / bruto − 1` as the per-SKU margin (it reproduces the
  documented 0.20–0.30 band exactly) and derive unit cost as `bruto / (1 + margin)`.
  Every margin number in the deliverable will carry this assumption explicitly.
- **Status**: open — awaiting VEMIO; fallback unblocked and documented

### F-001
**`product_margin` is required by Challenge B but absent from the delivered CSV — and is exactly recoverable**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (column inventory); resolved 2026-08-02 by F-003
- **Severity**: High → mitigated
- **Evidence**: The delivered CSV has **21 columns**; `product_margin` is not among them,
  though the dictionary describes it and Challenge B names it explicitly. F-003 then
  showed `product_cost / bruto − 1` is constant per SKU and lands inside the documented
  0.20–0.30 band, which is strong evidence that this ratio *is* the missing column.
- **Impact**: The margin component of the elasticity simulator can be built, but on a
  reconstructed column rather than a provided one — a disclosed assumption, not a fact.
- **Action taken**: Open question **Q1**; hypothesis **H-001** now `supported`.
- **Status**: mitigated — reconstruction validated, disclosure required in the report

### F-004
**`discount` is a fraction of list price, reconciles only at bundle level, and is negative on 2.8% of rows**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (reconciliation)
- **Severity**: High — it defines the effective price, Challenge B's independent variable
- **Evidence**: Values span **−0.9606 to 1.0** with a non-zero median of 0.159 and nothing
  above 1.0, so the column is a fraction, not percent-points. On the 128,977 promo rows
  that actually show a gross/net gap, the currency reading (`discount == bruto − net`)
  matches **0** rows, while the fraction reading (`discount == (bruto − net)/bruto`)
  matches **48.3%** within 0.001 — close but not exact, exactly as the dictionary's
  "calculated as bundle" note predicts. Distribution: 19,742 null (5.50%), 176,535 zero,
  **10,084 negative (2.81%)**, 152,414 positive.
- **Impact**: Effective unit price must be computed from `sell_in_amount / sell_in_quantity`
  rather than by applying `discount` to `bruto` — the latter would be wrong on more than
  half the promo rows. The negative values are unexplained and cannot be read as discounts.
- **Action taken**: Hypothesis **H-002** `supported`. Negative discounts raised as open
  question **Q6**; they will be flagged (not dropped) pending an answer.
- **Status**: open on the negative values; the unit-of-measure question is settled

### F-006
**Only two of the six SKUs carry enough realised price variation to support an elasticity estimate**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (price variation)
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Evidence**: Distinct realised unit prices per SKU — `1875` (Desodorante 150 ml A) with

  price movement or an artefact of bundle allocation within combos.
- **Action taken**: Feeds **H-004**; the SKU selection will get its own decision record in
  Phase 2 once the confound is checked.
- **Status**: open — selection deferred to Phase 2 by design

### F-007

- **Severity**: Info
- **Evidence**: 358,775 rows × 21 columns. 6 SKUs, 12 warehouses, 228 routes, 52,555

### F-002

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (intake)
- **Severity**: Info
- **Evidence**: SHA-256 recorded for all three delivered artifacts in
  [case/README.md](case/README.md). CSV: 80,513,309 bytes, `A8A9B8A3…6B68C0`. Every

- **Status**: closed
