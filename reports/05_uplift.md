## 1. How incrementality is identified

Episodes are derived from observed promotional intensity — contiguous runs of weeks where more than half of a SKU's units sold under a combo — rather than from combo identifiers. Several combos overlap on the same SKU, and what the business actually ran is the combined pressure, not any single code.

- **Baseline**: median weekly units over the six preceding weeks, restricted to weeks that were themselves quiet (under 20% promoted). A contaminated baseline would compare a promotion against another promotion.
- **Difference-in-differences**: the baseline is scaled by how much SKUs *not* promoted in the same weeks moved between the two windows. This absorbs market-wide shifts — seasonality, a strong month, a distribution push — that a raw before/after comparison would credit to the promotion.
- **Post-promotion window**: six weeks after. Volume that is repaid by a slump afterwards was pulled forward, not created. Net uplift subtracts it.

> The difference-in-differences estimate assumes the control SKUs would have moved in parallel with the promoted one absent the promotion. With six SKUs across three categories that assumption is doing real work and cannot be tested directly. Where the two estimates diverge sharply, the divergence itself is the finding — it means the answer depends on an assumption the data cannot settle.

## 5. Verdict

**Repeat: SKU 1283, the episode starting 2026-03-09** (3 weeks, average discount depth -0.0%). It produced 6,126 net incremental units against a counterfactual of 7,320, worth 235,479 in incremental margin, while the discount cost -146 on units that would have sold anyway — a net margin effect of 235,626. The post-promotion weeks show no offsetting slump.

**Do not repeat: SKU 1283, the episode starting 2025-06-02** (3 weeks, average discount depth 1.7%). It produced -4,244 net incremental units against a counterfactual of 2,851, worth -148,358 in incremental margin, while the discount cost 9,350 on units that would have sold anyway — a net margin effect of -157,708. Note the post-promotion slump: part of this volume was pulled forward rather than created.

## 5.1 The same promotion, opposite verdicts

SKUs 1665 and 1875 ran the same long-running promotion — both for 60 weeks, both at about 14% realised discount, both selling at almost exactly the same price (51.06 against 51.07). Both produced large volume uplift: 56,313 and 54,372 incremental units.

Yet one made money and the other lost it: **1665 nets 42,311** while **1875 nets -76,315**. The entire difference is the cost base. 1665 carries a 5.41 margin per unit at the promoted price; 1875 carries 2.37 — barely half. A discount deep enough to be profitable on the first product is deeper than the second can afford.

> **This is the single most transferable lesson in the analysis.** Uplift is not a measure of whether a promotion worked. Two products, the same mechanic, the same response, opposite outcomes — because promotional depth has to be set against each product's own margin, not applied uniformly across a portfolio. Both of these estimates are graded `weak` on identification, so the numbers carry error bars; the direction of the contrast does not depend on them.

## 6. What limits these numbers

- **The counterfactual is an assumption, not an observation.** There is no control group of clients who were not offered the promotion; the difference-in-differences control is other SKUs, which have their own demand drivers.
- **Cannibalisation is not measured.** A promotion on one shampoo may move volume from another rather than growing the category. Cross-SKU substitution during promoted weeks is the first extension worth building.
- **Six weeks may be too short to see the full pull-forward.** For a product bought monthly, the payback can land outside the window and make a promotion look better than it was.
- **Discount depth is realised, not offered.** It is inferred from gross versus net amounts, which F-004 showed reconcile only at bundle level; the true offer structure is not in the data.
- **Every margin figure inherits open question Q5.** Volume conclusions do not.
