### F-008
**The same promotion, run on two comparable SKUs, made money on one and lost it on the other**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4 (uplift economics)
- **Severity**: High — it is the finding the promotional recommendation rests on
- **Evidence**: SKUs 1665 and 1875 both ran the same long-running promotion: 60 weeks each,
  ~14% realised discount each, ending at almost identical prices (51.06 and 51.07). Both
  produced large volume uplift — **+56,313** and **+54,372** net incremental units. Yet
  1665 nets **+42,311** in margin while 1875 nets **−76,315**. The entire difference is the
  cost base: margin per unit at the promoted price is **5.41** for 1665 against **2.37** for
  1875, because their recovered margin rates are 0.30 and 0.22.
- **Impact**: Volume uplift is not a measure of whether a promotion worked. Promotional
  depth has to be set against each product's own margin rather than applied uniformly
  across a portfolio. This drives business recommendation #1.
- **Confidence**: Both estimates are graded `weak` on identification — no clean control SKU
  existed in those weeks — so the magnitudes carry error bars. The *direction* of the
  contrast does not depend on the counterfactual, because both SKUs share it.
- **Status**: closed

### F-009
**The promotions that created margin were the ones with no discount at all**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4
- **Severity**: High
- **Evidence**: SKU 1283's March 2026 episode ran at a realised discount depth of **−0.0%**
  — a placement or bundling mechanic rather than a price cut — and produced **6,126 net
  incremental units** for **+235,626** in margin, the best result of the nine episodes
  measured. Discount-led episodes on the *same SKU* produced comparable volume
  (+6,155 and +2,736 units) but lost **−47,595** and **−67,479**, because the discount is
  paid on every unit sold in the window including the ones that would have sold anyway.
- **Impact**: Drives business recommendation #2 — build the calendar around non-price
  mechanics first and make depth discounting justify itself.
- **Confidence**: The March 2026 episode is graded `moderate` (clean baseline, observable
  post window, no control SKUs available). The pattern across episodes is consistent enough
  that the conclusion does not rest on any single one.
- **Status**: closed

### F-010
**No sophisticated model beat a 4-week moving average on two of the three forecast SKUs**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4/5
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Evidence**: Across five rolling origins at a 12-week horizon, the moving average won on
  SKU 1857 (WAPE **0.257**) and 1665 (**0.291**), beating ETS, harmonic ridge, seasonal
  naive and an ensemble. Only SKU 1283 supported a better model: damped drift at **0.272**
  against the best baseline's 0.322, a 15.5% improvement. Seasonal naive was among the worst
  performers everywhere (see H-003).
- **Impact**: Sets the realistic accuracy expectation for planning (~26–29% weekly error)
  and justifies not shipping a complex model. Also produced the diagnostic that led to the
  damped-drift candidate: every level-only model under-forecast by 13–30%, revealing an
  upward drift that flat forecasts could not follow.
- **Status**: closed

### F-011
**Roughly a third of SKU 1665's history was priced below break-even**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-03, Phase 4 (simulator)
- **Severity**: High
- **Evidence**: Under the reconstructed unit cost (46.30, from F-003), the simulator's
  margin curve crosses zero at a price of about **47**. The realised weekly price fell below
  that line in roughly a third of the 72 weeks observed, with the deepest weeks at 42.87 —
  an **−8% margin**. Demand is genuinely elastic (−4.73), so those weeks did sell more
  volume; each additional unit was sold at a loss.
- **Impact**: Gives the commercial team a hard price floor and drives business
  recommendation #5. Also explains why the revenue-maximising price sits at the boundary of
  the observed range — a corner solution the simulator correctly refuses to extrapolate past.
- **Caveat**: Inherits open question **Q5**. If VEMIO's cost reading differs, the break-even
  price moves; the elasticity and volume conclusions do not.
- **Status**: open pending Q5

### F-003
**`product_cost` sits above gross revenue on every row — the margin is applied in the wrong direction**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (cost/margin structure)
- **Severity**: **Critical** — taken literally, the client loses money on all 358,775 transactions
- **Evidence**: The ratio `product_cost / bruto` is **exactly constant within each SKU**
  (standard deviation 0.0 to nine decimals):

  | product_code | product_name | cost/bruto | implied margin | margin as literally delivered |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1283 | Cubito de pollo c/50 | 1.24 | 0.24 | **−19.4%** |
  | 1665 | Antitranspirante 150 ml C | 1.30 | 0.30 | **−23.1%** |
  | 1857 | Shampoo Rizos 135 ml | 1.27 | 0.27 | **−21.3%** |
  | 1858 | Shampoo 135 ml Azul | 1.26 | 0.26 | **−20.6%** |
  | 1875 | Desodorante 150 ml A | 1.22 | 0.22 | **−18.0%** |
  | 9304 | Shampoo 180ml Verde | 1.22 | 0.22 | **−18.0%** |

  The `cost/bruto − 1` column lands on 0.22–0.30 — precisely the "fixed markup per SKU,
  between 0.20 and 0.30, constant across the period" that the dictionary attributes to
  `product_margin`. The relationship in the file is `product_cost = bruto × (1 + margin)`,
  whereas a markup over cost implies `product_cost = bruto ÷ (1 + margin)`.
- **Impact**: Every margin figure in Challenge B depends on which reading is intended.
  Using the column as delivered would report a loss-making business and make the
  price-optimisation exercise meaningless; correcting it silently would hide a material
  assumption behind the headline recommendation.
- **Action taken**: Raised as open question **Q5** to VEMIO. Documented fallback: treat
  `product_margin = product_cost / bruto − 1` as the per-SKU margin (it reproduces the
  documented 0.20–0.30 band exactly) and derive unit cost as `bruto / (1 + margin)`.
  Every margin number in the deliverable will carry this assumption explicitly.
- **Status**: open — awaiting VEMIO; fallback unblocked and documented

### F-001
**`product_margin` is required by Challenge B but absent from the delivered CSV — and is exactly recoverable**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (column inventory); resolved 2026-08-02 by F-003
- **Severity**: High → mitigated
- **Evidence**: The delivered CSV has **21 columns**; `product_margin` is not among them,
  though the dictionary describes it and Challenge B names it explicitly. F-003 then
  showed `product_cost / bruto − 1` is constant per SKU and lands inside the documented
  0.20–0.30 band, which is strong evidence that this ratio *is* the missing column.
- **Impact**: The margin component of the elasticity simulator can be built, but on a
  reconstructed column rather than a provided one — a disclosed assumption, not a fact.
- **Action taken**: Open question **Q1**; hypothesis **H-001** now `supported`.
- **Status**: mitigated — reconstruction validated, disclosure required in the report

### F-004
**`discount` is a fraction of list price, reconciles only at bundle level, and is negative on 2.8% of rows**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (reconciliation)
- **Severity**: High — it defines the effective price, Challenge B's independent variable
- **Evidence**: Values span **−0.9606 to 1.0** with a non-zero median of 0.159 and nothing
  above 1.0, so the column is a fraction, not percent-points. On the 128,977 promo rows
  that actually show a gross/net gap, the currency reading (`discount == bruto − net`)
  matches **0** rows, while the fraction reading (`discount == (bruto − net)/bruto`)
  matches **48.3%** within 0.001 — close but not exact, exactly as the dictionary's
  "calculated as bundle" note predicts. Distribution: 19,742 null (5.50%), 176,535 zero,
  **10,084 negative (2.81%)**, 152,414 positive.
- **Impact**: Effective unit price must be computed from `sell_in_amount / sell_in_quantity`
  rather than by applying `discount` to `bruto` — the latter would be wrong on more than
  half the promo rows. The negative values are unexplained and cannot be read as discounts.
- **Action taken**: Hypothesis **H-002** `supported`. Negative discounts raised as open
  question **Q6**; they will be flagged (not dropped) pending an answer.
- **Status**: open on the negative values; the unit-of-measure question is settled

### F-006
**Only two of the six SKUs carry enough realised price variation to support an elasticity estimate**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1 (price variation)
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Evidence**: Distinct realised unit prices per SKU — `1875` (Desodorante 150 ml A) with
  74 list / 494 net prices and `1665` (Antitranspirante 150 ml C) with 78 / 493 are an
  order of magnitude richer than `9304` (9 / 38), `1857` (11 / 79) and `1858` (12 / 62).
  `1283` sits in between (20 / 125).
- **Impact**: Challenge B's SKU choice is now a documented criterion rather than a
  convenient one. It also flags a confound to check: whether that variation is genuine
  price movement or an artefact of bundle allocation within combos.
- **Action taken**: Feeds **H-004**; the SKU selection will get its own decision record in
  Phase 2 once the confound is checked.
- **Status**: open — selection deferred to Phase 2 by design

### F-005

### F-007
**Dataset shape matches the case statement; weekly coverage is complete**

- **Discovered**: 2026-08-02, Phase 1
- **Severity**: Info
- **Evidence**: 358,775 rows × 21 columns. 6 SKUs, 12 warehouses, 228 routes, 52,555
  clients, 273,779 tickets, 79 combos — all consistent with the stated "6 SKUs, 12
  warehouses, ~52,500 clients, ~359,000 transactions, 79 combos". Dates parse without
  failure as `dd/mm/yyyy`, verified against the independent `year`/`month` columns
  (358,775 of 358,775 agree, and days above 12 are present, so the format is not
  ambiguous). Coverage runs 2025-01-02 → 2026-05-30: **74 weeks spanned, 74 observed,
  0 missing**, across 438 distinct days. The claimed grain holds — zero duplicate rows at
  `date × client × product × ticket × combo`. 52.76% of rows (189,282) were sold inside a combo.
- **Impact**: Weekly aggregation for Challenge A needs no gap-filling, and the promo/organic
  split is close to balanced, which is favourable for the uplift work in Challenge C.
- **Status**: closed
